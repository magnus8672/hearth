Why hearth exists

Intelligence belongs
close to home.

hearth is built on the belief that inference belongs at the edge: in our homes, on our devices, where our conversations can stay between us and our families.

We see AI as the most pivotal wave of technological possibility since the dot-com boom. Keeping up with it should be within reach, without paying hundreds of dollars every month simply to participate.

That vision includes paying model makers for a license to run their work on hardware we own. Creators deserve compensation; users deserve the choice to operate locally, with control over privacy, ongoing costs and continued access.

The ambition is personal ownership: use our own compute, choose our own models, and give the people around us a place to learn, experiment and create. A lifelong hosted subscription should be a choice.

03 / Built around your farm

A little coordination.
A lot of possibility.

The head does not need a GPU. It brings identity, routing and the workspace together while model hosts do the inference.

Read the architecture decision ↗
A

Independent machines, independent work.

Keep specialists resident on separate hosts so chat and media can run concurrently. Connect more than one instance of a provider and bind it to the capabilities it can serve.

B

One GPU? Share it deliberately.

In managed shared-media mode, image and 3D jobs take turns fairly across owners. The worker switches the selected backend when scheduled. Independent chat capacity stays independent.

C

A personal workspace, a visible farm.

Private chats and memory have account boundaries; channels are shared with their members. Administrators get a separate view of connections, readiness and observed model residency.

An honest progress report

Working software.
Still being built.

hearth has bounded implementations across all fourteen capability profiles. That is feature coverage, not a completed production release.

What works, and what is missing ↗

The next layer is qualification.

Signed installers, guided node enrollment, broader hardware and load testing, and complete upgrade and recovery qualification remain release work. The universal text/audio queue and paid cloud gateway are not implemented.

The current build defaults to local-only operation and a zero cloud budget. Provider support and readiness must be verified for your configuration.
